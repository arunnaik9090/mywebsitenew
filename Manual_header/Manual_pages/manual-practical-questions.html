Manual Practical Questions

Practice turning realistic product situations into focused, risk-based test ideas.

1. Test a login form

Cover valid and invalid credentials, required fields, whitespace, case sensitivity where relevant, locked or disabled accounts, session behavior, password masking, and accessible error messages. Check that authentication failures do not disclose whether an account exists.

2. Test a shopping cart quantity field

Check minimum and maximum quantities, zero, negative and non-numeric input, stock limits, repeated updates, price recalculation, persistence after refresh, and behavior when inventory changes during checkout.

3. Test a file upload

Check allowed and disallowed types, size boundaries, empty files, duplicate names, interrupted uploads, progress feedback, safe filename handling, and whether unauthorized users can access uploaded content.

4. A defect cannot be reproduced

Confirm the reported build, account, data, environment, and exact steps. Ask for timestamps and sanitized evidence, check logs and dependencies, and try nearby input variations. Record what was tried instead of closing the issue without a trace.

5. A release is due today and a critical test is blocked

Communicate the blocker and its risk immediately, identify a safe alternative or dependency owner, assess related coverage, and present the remaining uncertainty to the release decision-maker. Do not report blocked coverage as passed.

How to sustain QA job in AI world

  1. Shift from repetitive execution to risk-based thinking and test strategy.
  2. Learn how to use AI tools to improve productivity without depending on them blindly.
  3. Strengthen business domain knowledge so you validate the product from a user-impact viewpoint.
  4. Master exploratory testing, edge cases, and real-world user journeys.
  5. Build strong API, database, and integration testing skills.
  6. Focus on testing the critical path, not just chasing every low-value scenario.
  7. Improve automation skills beyond record-and-playback to framework and architecture understanding.
  8. Develop communication skills to explain defects, risks, and release confidence clearly.
  9. Gain knowledge in security, performance, accessibility, and compliance testing.
  10. Become the QA who connects product risk, quality data, and engineering decisions.
